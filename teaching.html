Teaching, outreach, and code

Hands-on math

I like teaching mathematics with real problems and real data: a jellyfish pulse, a heart rhythm, a horse's stride. Most of what we build for teaching is free to use.

Research with students

  • Horse and Math Innovation Lab. A Vertically Integrated Project where students from many majors work together for several semesters on horse movement and physiology.
  • Course-based research in equine biomechanics. A class where every student does original research: collecting data, analyzing it, and building models.
  • Summer REU. Our Research Training Group in Data Driven Discovery, with support from the Provost Investment Fund, runs a summer program that pairs mathematics and data science with equine science.
  • Independent study. MATH 199 to 499, at any level.

Code

Most of our simulations use the immersed boundary method, first developed by Charles Peskin. These are the tools we use and share.

MATLAB and Python

IB2d

An easy-to-use immersed boundary code in 2D with dozens of examples: springs, beams, muscles, porous structures, and advection-diffusion. Led by Nick Battista.

github.com/nickabattista/IB2d
C++, parallel

IBAMR

Boyce Griffith's adaptive, parallel immersed boundary library for 3D problems. We share our tutorials, slides, and example models.

Using IB Software tutorialsIBAMR on GitHub
